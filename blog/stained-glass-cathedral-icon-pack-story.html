Sunlight Through Lead: Making the Stained Glass Icon Pack

Sunlight in a cathedral window turns plain glass into colour. We tried to do that on a phone screen.

By Maurice Moret · November 2026 · 3 min read

A stained glass window only looks alive when the sun is behind it. A phone screen is lit from behind all day, which made it an oddly perfect place for the idea. Stained Glass gives each of its 2,900+ icons the look of a tiny leaded window.

Panes, lead and bloom

Every symbol is cut from separate pieces of glass divided by thin lines of dark lead, the way a window maker would assemble it. The glass shows hammered ripples, a few trapped bubbles and a soft bloom where light spills over the lead, so each icon looks lit from behind. The symbol sits on a darker leaded plate and glows against it, and a frame of solid lead came runs around each plate.

Three plates for a cathedral

A window of one repeated pattern looks like wallpaper, so the plates alternate between three: a quarry lattice of small diamond panes, bullseye roundels, or plain lead with a heavy border. The lead lines had to be thin enough to keep small symbols readable and thick enough to read as lead, and that balance took many rounds. The bloom comes last: a faint glow that spreads from the glass over the lead, which is what sells the idea that the light comes from behind.

What is inside

  • 2,900+ icons in one style, in ten jewel tones: ruby, sapphire, emerald, amber, amethyst, teal, rose, citrine, smoke and ivory.
  • A fallback tile for apps that have no icon of their own yet, a plain lead plate, on launchers that let an icon pack supply one, so the grid has no gaps.
  • 12 matching wallpapers, made to go with the icons. Set one for the home screen, the lock screen or both from the Wallpapers tab.
  • Dark and light mode, with a toggle in the app or following your phone, and search across the whole set.
  • An icon request screen. Tap Send and the list of apps you see, plus an email if you type one, goes straight to the developer. That is the only time the app goes online.
  • No ads, no analytics and no subscription. It is a one-time purchase of EUR 1.99 on Google Play.

All the symbols are generic. They are drawn by our own tools, and none of them copies a company logo, so no app is promised an exact match. Apps get a symbol that suits what they do, in a colour close to their own.

Twelve wallpapers to match

An icon pack looks best on a wallpaper that belongs to it, so Stained Glass comes with twelve. They share the colours and the mood of the icons, and you can set any of them from the app without installing anything else.

How to use it

  1. Install a launcher that supports icon packs, such as Nova Launcher, Lawnchair, Niagara, Smart Launcher, Apex Launcher or ADW Launcher. Your current one may already.
  2. Open Stained Glass and go to the Apply tab. Pick your launcher for a one-tap apply, or open the launcher settings and choose Stained Glass as the icon pack.
  3. Later, after the pack has been updated, tap Refresh icons in the app to apply the new set again. If the launcher still shows old icons, restart it.

On a Samsung phone? Samsung's own launcher, One UI Home, does not support icon packs, so install one of the launchers above first and set it as your home app. The app has a short guide that walks you through it.
